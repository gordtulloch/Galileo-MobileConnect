# Changelog

All notable changes to this repository are recorded here, in [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) format.

## [Unreleased]

### Changed

- **The Solve screen's menu hides and shows as a whole; the per-pane checklist is gone.** One button on the picture hides the menu (Solver Control, Latest result and the polar-alignment panes) so the picture has the screen, and brings it back; the choice is kept on the device. Choosing which panes to show is no longer offered.

- **The app is named just *Galileo* on the iOS home screen**, in the welcome screen's title and in the Settings path the Local Network help gives (it was *Galileo MobileConnect*). The bundle identifier is unchanged, so an installed copy updates in place.

- **The Solve screen no longer has a *Centre on a position* pane.** It asked for raw RA/Dec numbers; centring is done from the Sky screen's *Goto and Centre*, which takes a chosen object. Capture & Solve, Load & Slew and polar alignment are unchanged, and `solve.centre` is still offered by the Master.

- **The app icon is now the standard Galileo icon** (the gold G with the telescope over a galaxy, the same artwork as the desktop's `galileo.icns`), replacing the placeholder red telescope. iOS needs a square with no transparency, so the artwork is cropped to its edge and the rounded corners are filled with its deep navy; iOS rounds it again.

### Fixed

- **Capture & Solve now shows the frame being solved, and no longer reports "a plate solve is already running" while its own solve runs.** The Solve screen switches to the solve's frame as soon as the Master announces it (`solve.frame.version`), not once there is a result, and an operation button stops showing its "unavailable because" note while the operation it started is the one in progress. Needs a Galileo with the same change.

- **The red "Galileo's control surface is not running" bar stayed after Galileo came up.** The phone only asked for the Piers when it (re)connected, and the stream does not drop when the Master's plugin is up but Galileo's control surface is not yet, so a Galileo that started or restarted later never cleared it. While that notice is showing the app now asks again every five seconds, and when the Piers appear it takes their catalogues and the live state and drops the bar.

- **The second caption under the polar/solve bullseye ("rings 30″ × 1, 2, 3") was cut off at the bottom.** The plot now leaves room for both lines.

### Added

- **The Solve screen now carries the desktop's controls and results.** A **Solver Control** pane shows the bullseye (each solution's offset from its target in arcseconds, with rings at 1, 2 and 3 times the accuracy, newest brightest), the exposure field, **Capture & Solve**, **Load & Slew…** (choose one of the Library's recent frames on the Master to solve and slew to) and **Stop**, and below them the **Solver Action** radio buttons: Sync, Slew to Target (the default; it aims at the Pier's current object) and Nothing. The choice is kept on the device. **Latest result** now shows Solution Coordinates (JNow) as the desktop does: RA, DE, Err (with dRA and dDE), Pix, PA, FOV, R, FL and F/, the solved value with the optics' prediction in brackets. The panes can be hidden as before. The old separate Solve here pane is merged into Solver Control. Needs core's `CSURF-140` additions (`action` on `solve.capture`, `solve.load`, `solve.frames`, and the published `solve.*` state); on an older Master the buttons say the Master does not offer them. The mock Master serves the same shape. iOS only; **built and unit tests pass, but not seen on a device or simulator**.

- **Polar alignment now refreshes the picture in a loop and draws the circle, cross and vector on it.** While polar alignment is adjusting, the Solve screen's frame is the frame just solved (the Master's new `solve` preview source), fetched again at once after each solve, with a circle where the mount's axis points, a cross at the celestial pole, and an arrow from the circle to the cross labelled with how far it is (for example 3′12″). Move the axis so the cross comes into the circle. The marks are drawn inside the zoomed picture, so they stay on the stars when you pinch or pan and keep their size on screen, and they use the theme's colours, so Night stays red. They are shown only for the frame they were computed for; if a newer picture arrives first they wait for it. The same frame source now also shows the frame of **Take a frame and solve** on that screen. API: `source=solve` on `preview` and `tile`. The mock Master's scripted run carries the geometry, so the demo shows the vector shrinking. Needs core's `CSURF-145` frame; on an older Master the screen keeps showing the latest exposure with no marks. iOS only; **built and unit tests pass, but not yet seen on a device or simulator, and the vector's direction has not been checked against a real mount** (the pixel convention is from the solver's CD matrix, as the desktop's aiming view uses).

### Changed

- **The Solve screen shows the latest frame on the left with its panes stacked on the right, and any pane can be hidden.** The frame is the same picture the Imaging screen shows (pinch to zoom), and it refreshes after each solve and each polar-alignment refresh. The panes are Latest result, Solve here, Centre on a position, Polar alignment and Polar error. The **Panes** button on the picture opens a checklist to hide or show each one (and **Show all**); the choice is kept on the device. Hide all but Polar error (and Polar alignment, to stop it) and the prompts, the large error figure and the aiming plot have the whole column; hide every pane and the frame has the screen. On a phone the frame sits above the panes. The button stays on the picture, so the panes can always be brought back. iOS only, built and compiled; **not yet looked at on a device or simulator**.

### Added

- **Master computer health, with Restart and Shut down, on the Overview screen (`MAPP-OVR-080`).** A new *Master computer* card shows CPU use, memory use, free disk space, temperature (when the Master's computer reports one) and uptime, updating every few seconds from the Master's `node.health` state, and a line when a figure is tight or critical. This is the quickest way to learn, from the telescope, that a Raspberry Pi's card is nearly full or it is running hot. *Restart* and *Shut down* ask for the Master's confirmation before doing anything, and the Master refuses them (the app shows why) while a session is running. A Master older than this change shows "Waiting for the Master's health report…" and "This Master doesn't offer this yet." instead. The mock Master and demonstration mode carry the new operations and a sample report; the health values go stale in the app if the Master stops sending them (60 s). Needs Galileo with `CSURF-310`. Tests: `StateTests` (freshness of the report).

- **Choose the guide star by tapping, and see the whole guide frame (`MAPP-GUI-020`, `MAPP-GUI-030`).** The Guiding screen has a new *Guide star* card with a *Star* / *Full frame* switch. *Star* draws PHD2's close-up of the guide star (the app decodes `guiding.star`, which until now it did not draw); *Full frame* shows the whole frame PHD2 is guiding on, from `GET /preview?source=guide`, refreshed every two seconds while it is on screen. Tapping either picture while PHD2 is looping sends `guiding.select_star` with where the tap landed; the Master finds the best star in a small box there. Taps while guiding are not sent, and the screen says so. `openapi.yaml` gains `guide` as a preview source. The Master can only supply the frame when it can read the file PHD2 saves, so the card says so when there is none. Needs a Galileo and MobileConnect plugin with the same change.

- **Polar alignment on the Solve screen (`MAPP-SOL-030`).** Start and stop the routine, and watch it as you turn the mount's altitude and azimuth knobs: phase and step, the total error as a large figure coloured by quality (good under 1′, fair under 5′, poor above), the correction for each adjuster in words, a plot of the pole (cross) against the point the axis aims at (circle), and a trend line showing whether the error is falling. Everything follows the Master's `polar.state` (core `CSURF-145`) as it is published, with no polling. On a Master that does not offer `polar.*` the controls say so. The mock Master has matching `polar.start` and `polar.stop` effects and the `polar-alignment` scenario now uses the real state shape (the earlier proposal used `polar.alt_error_arcmin` and a string `polar.state`; nothing else read those). Built for iOS only (the Android client has no screens yet); **not yet run against a real Master, mount or iPad**. The last-frame preview during alignment is not wired in.

### Changed

- **"Lease" is now "control" wherever a person reads it.** The top bar says *Control: yours*, *Take control* and *Control: <who>*, and the dialog says *Control is held*; refusals say "X has control of this Pier" and "Take control of this Pier first". Code, operation names and the wire format still say lease. The Master's own refusal messages changed to match (core's gateway and the plugin), so an older Master will still show the old words in a refusal.
- **The survey is now Mellinger's Milky Way mosaic, and zoomed in past a 3° field it fetches nothing new.** The Master's Digitized Sky Survey tiles were patchy (plate seams, differing backgrounds); Mellinger's is one continuous picture, with tiles to order 4. Deep in, the view draws the tiles it already holds, so panning there no longer waits on the network.

### Added

- **Three more mock Master scenarios for demonstration mode: `focus-run`, `polar-alignment` and `session-run`.** The demo and the standalone mock Master only had a normal night, a safety stop and a flip, so a reviewer or first-time user could not see a focus run, a polar alignment or a whole session without knowing which buttons to press. Each new scenario plays by itself from the first stream connection: an autofocus V-curve filling in to a result; three solves then an altitude/azimuth error that shrinks to an aligned result (new `polar.*` state keys, a proposal for `PierState` since core has no polar-alignment operation yet); and a session stepping through slew, centre, focus and a 30-frame series to completion. Run one with `--scenario <name>` on the mock server. Data only, no player changes; `MockMasterTests` lists all 15.
- **Live stacking from the app.** A *Live stack* switch in the capture settings asks the Master to stack a series of three or more frames. While it builds, a *Live stack* bar along the bottom shows frames stacked out of those asked for and the integration time so far, a separate *Exposure* bar counts down the frame being taken, and a *Show stack* checkbox above the bars chooses between the stack (checked, and ticked for you as soon as a stack starts) and the latest exposure. The stack is rendered by the Master like any frame, so stretch, zoom and deep-zoom tiles work on it. The preview and tile requests take a `source` (`current` or `stack`) in `api/openapi.yaml`. The demo and mock Masters publish no stack.
- **Imaging gets the whole screen.** The current frame now fills the Imaging screen, as the sky does on the Sky screen. Exposure, count, frame type, gain and offset are in a panel that slides down from a button at the top right, with their boxes side by side instead of one per row; stretch, single exposure, abort and the live loop are behind *More*. Exposure progress (frame x of n) and a live-stack bar are drawn along the bottom of the picture, and star count, HFR and FWHM as a small caption. `OperationForm` takes the compact layout wherever compact controls are set.
- **The sky gets the whole screen.** Search, time and display are now in a panel that slides over the sky from a button at the top right (up from the bottom on a phone), with smaller controls. Tapping empty sky closes it, and *Keep open* keeps it beside the sky instead. A label on the sky says when the time shown isn't now.
- **Object menu, as on the Star Atlas.** Tapping an object or choosing a search result opens a menu beside it, with Goto, Goto and Centre, Sync and Add to Session. Add to Session takes the target to the Sessions screen, where saved sessions run on it.
- **Sky survey and catalog choices in the Sky panel.** Sky survey and Constellations switches, and a Catalog choice of Messier, Caldwell and NGC, starting with Messier as the Star Atlas does.
- **Real sky behind the stars.** The app draws Digitized Sky Survey photographs from the Master, tile by tile at a resolution matching the zoom, so the Milky Way, nebulae and galaxies are simply there and sharpen as you zoom in. True colour on the Dark theme, red only in Night, not on the Light theme, with the credit on the sky and a switch in the panel. Tiles stay in memory only. The demo and both mock Masters have none, as they aren't ours to ship.
- **Landscape ground and a red horizon fill.** Everything below 0° altitude is hidden behind an opaque, made-up landscape, red only in Night, with a Ground switch in the panel; objects there can't be tapped, and the selection and mount markers still show. What the local horizon profile blocks, from 0° up, is filled in transparent red.
- **Sky view.** The sky above the Observatory, drawn with Metal from data streamed from the Master, brightest first: stars and deep-sky objects, constellation figures and names (with a switch), the Sun, Moon and planets, the horizon profile, the mount's position and its field of view. Pan, pinch and tap; search by name; move through the night and back to now. A go-to asks first, naming the target, its altitude and the Master's horizon check. With the link down it says so and hides the mount's position. The app ships no catalog and computes no ephemeris.
- **Live loop and deep zoom.** One exposure after another, each shown as it arrives, only while the app is in front. Zooming the image loads sharper tiles from the Master. Stretch is one control.
- **Release files.** A privacy manifest declaring no tracking and no data collected, an app icon (a first version), and a draft `LICENSE` for review. The App Store listing itself is still to do.
- The Sky screen tells "this Observatory has no location" (set it on the desktop) from "couldn't get the sky data" (try again). They looked the same at first, which sent someone to look for a setting that wasn't the problem.
- Sky routes in the draft plugin and both mock Masters. `api/vectors/sky/` holds a frame written by core's own encoder, which the Swift decoder is tested against.
- **Solve, Flip and Sessions screens.** Plate solve, sync and centre with the result shown; the flip's state and time remaining, and a flip after the Master's confirmation; running, pausing, resuming and stopping a saved session, with block position and progress, and the job queue. Controls for what the server has no operation for yet (skip a step, defer a flip) say so.
- **Tested end to end against the real server code.** `tools/mock-master/real_link_server.py` runs Galileo's real Connect Link listener, Gateway and lease with the draft MobileConnect plugin (in `Galileo-Plugins/mobileconnect`) and fake devices, and the real client pairs, pins, takes the lease, operates, confirms a park, holds a control and uses an emergency stop against it. `scripts/check_ios.sh` runs this when the checkouts are alongside.
- **iOS app, first working version.** A universal iPhone/iPad app that finds or is given a Master's address, pairs (showing the code, with the certificate pinned and the token in the Keychain), reconnects by itself, and shows the Overview, Equipment, Imaging, Focus, Guiding and Safety screens plus Settings. Every control is built from the Master's operation catalogue, shows the Master's own answer, and marks a stale value as such. An Operator gets the lease, confirmations and held motion; a Viewer gets no controls. Sky, Solve, Flip, Sessions, Schedule and Library are not built: `docs/CHECKLIST.md` section 5a lists what the MVP still needs.
- **Demonstration mode** ("Try the demo"): the real client code against a scripted Master inside the app, labelled on every screen, so the app can be tried with no observatory and App Review has something to look at.
- **Emergency actions on every screen**: stop everything, park and close dome, in two interactions, with a one-second press-and-hold, needing no lease, and listing each part's result.
- **Night theme that is provably red.** Colours come from `design/tokens.json` (contrast and red-only are checked by `scripts/gen_tokens.py`), and every Night screenshot from the UI tests is checked pixel by pixel by `scripts/check_night.py`. Night is the default on first launch.
- **`GalileoCore`**: the connection, API, live-state and control-safety logic, in plain Swift with no UI, specified in `docs/SDD.md` for the Kotlin side to implement too.
- **Shared test vectors** in `api/vectors/`, now 15, with explicit `act` steps so that any platform's runner can replay them with no knowledge of individual files. They cover pairing, stream replay and resynchronisation, outcome-unknown, confirmation, typed refusals, revocation, Viewers, emergency actions and held motion.
- **Mock Master scenarios** in `api/scenarios/` (normal night, safety event, meridian flip, lease contention, dropped stream, revoked pairing, refused pairing, Viewer, Pi-class Master, older and newer Master, certificate change), played by the in-process player and by a **standalone server**, `tools/mock-master/mock_master.py`, which speaks real HTTPS and WebSocket. The catalogue they use is dumped from a real Galileo checkout.
- `scripts/check_ios.sh` runs all of the above, and refuses literal colours and system controls that can't be held to Night in `ios/App`.
- **iOS tooling.** The Swift client generates from `api/openapi.yaml` and builds with its dependencies pinned. The Xcode project is generated from `ios/project.yml`. The bundle identifier is `com.gordtulloch.galileo.mobileconnect` (PSD Q5).

### Changed

- **The Sky view's projection is now exactly the Star Atlas's.** The field of view is measured up the view's height rather than across its shorter side, which made portrait views a different scale from Galileo; J2000 positions are precessed to the date, which moved everything by about 0.37 degrees; and the sidereal time, far limit, zoom limits, altitude limits and pan rate now match. `api/vectors/sky/projection.json` is written by Galileo's own code, and the tests hold the app to it.
- **The iPad's menu takes much less room.** It's only as wide as its longest label, and it now hides itself: a button in the top bar opens it and shows which screen you're on, and choosing a screen or tapping away closes it. **Keep open** pins it beside the screen instead, and is remembered. The system navigation bar is gone too, so screens gain its height.
- Tapping a star no longer asks for deep-sky details, which only left a refusal in the Master's audit log.
- The welcome screen says what to try if nothing has been found after 10 seconds, instead of only showing a spinner. Bonjour took about 30 seconds on the first real iPad, on a mesh Wi-Fi network.
- **The wire contract now matches what Connect Link can do**, found by writing the plugin against it (`docs/SDD.md` 4.2, items 12-15). Routes are flat with query parameters, because its route table matches exact paths. The client asks for its snapshot, because the stream has no connection hook. The preview has one stretch value, as `galileo.preview` does, so the app's three sliders became one. A lease's holder has a name to show. Typed outcomes have fixed HTTP statuses, never 5xx.
- **Design documents corrected against what the server really does** (`docs/SDD.md` 4.2, items 8-11). Role comes from `whoami`, because the handshake's `allowed` always includes `config.*` and would have locked the app out of every real Master. Emergency operations use core's real names. A handshake answering `restricted` only because the app reports no database level proceeds without showing its message. Item 11 lists the MVP operations core doesn't have yet.
- A value now ages out only if it moves continuously, because the Master sends a value only when it changes: otherwise "tracking: yes" would turn stale after a few seconds of nothing happening.

### Fixed

- The standalone mock Master advertises on Bonjour on a Mac where binding every interface fails with "address in use", as it did on a link-local address.
- **API description, corrected against the Galileo source.** A 403 is now described as plain text, as the server sends it; described as JSON, the generated client would have thrown a decoding error instead of reporting "forbidden". `transferGetChunk` gains its 404 and 503 (busy, with `Retry-After`) responses, and `transferStatus` its 400. `Operation.class` is no longer an enum, so an unknown class drops one entry rather than failing the whole catalogue.
